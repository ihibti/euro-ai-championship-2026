# Brief: Pixel Seal — Adversarial-only training for invisible image and video watermarking

Souček, Fernandez, Elsahar, Rebuffi, Lacatusu, Tran, Sander, Mourachko (Meta FAIR). arXiv [2512.16874v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16874), Dec 2025.
PDF: `papers/2512.16874v1_pixel-seal.pdf` (fetch with `make papers`).

This paper is one of three organizer references. We do **not** assume it defines the challenge. Sections 1–4 report the authors' work. Sections 5b and 8 are our assessment and ideas.

## 1. Problem

The paper addresses post-hoc, multi-bit, invisible watermarking. An embedder hides an n-bit message in an existing image or video, and an extractor recovers it after the content has been edited. The authors identify three issues with prior methods (§1, §4):

1. **Proxy losses leave visible artifacts.** Perceptual proxy losses (MSE, LPIPS) still produce visible artifacts.
2. **Robustness and imperceptibility conflict.** The two objectives pull against each other, which makes training unstable and dependent on tuning.
3. **Upscaling causes artifacts.** Models trained at 256² and upscaled to high resolution show artifacts and a train/test shift.

## 2. Main method

- **Framework (§3.1, Eq. 1).** `x_w = x + α·w`, where `w = Emb(x, m) ∈ [−1,1]^{H×W×3}`. The extractor outputs soft bits `m̃ ∈ [0,1]^n`, which are then thresholded.
- **Architecture (§5.1).**
  - Embedder: U-Net (43.8M parameters). Extractor: ConvNeXt-v2 Tiny (33.4M).
  - **n_bits = 256.**
  - Training: 600k steps at batch 256 on SA-1b, AdamW with lr 5e-4, cosine schedule, 20k warmup steps.
- **Adversarial-only objective (§4.1, Eq. 6).**
  - The only losses are message BCE (Eq. 3) and a patch discriminator with a hinge loss (from Rombach et al. 2022). There is no MSE or LPIPS term.
  - λ_msg = 1.0 and λ_adv = 0.1.
  - "Boosting" sets `x̂_w = x + β(x_w − x)`. The released model uses β = 1.
- **JND attenuation (Eq. 7).** `x_w = x + α·(w ⊙ JND(x))`, where the JND map is a fixed heatmap with values in [0,1].
- **Three-stage schedule (§4.2, Eq. 8).**
  1. Stage 1 trains on the message loss only, with α₀ = 1.0.
  2. Stage 2 adds the adversarial loss and anneals α with `α(t) = α₁ + (α₀−α₁)·cos(π/2·φ)`, where φ is the clipped training progress and α₁ = 0.2.
  3. Stage 3 fine-tunes at α₁.
  - **The paper does not give N_start, N_end or the length of stage 1.**
- **High-resolution adaptation (§4.3, Eq. 10).** Training simulates the inference pipeline:
  - The image is downscaled before the embedder, and the watermark is upscaled and applied at full resolution with the JND map.
  - The attack runs at full resolution, and the result is downscaled before the extractor.
  - Training image sides are sampled from [256, 768].
- **Non-differentiable attacks (Eq. 5).** These use a straight-through estimator: `x̃_w = x_w + nograd(T(x_w) − x_w)`.
- **Temporal watermark pooling (§4.4).** At inference only, the U-Net does a temporal average-pool after down-block d and a repeat-unpool at the matching up-block. It needs no video training.

## 3. Experimental setup (§5.1)

- **Evaluation data.**
  - 1000 Meta AI–generated images at 1280². The paper does not say whether they are released.
  - 100 SA-1b validation images.
  - 121 MovieGen and 96 SA-V videos.
- **Baselines.** CIN, InvisMark, MBRS, TrustMark-P/Q, Video Seal 0.0/1.0, WAM, RivaGAN.
- **Attacks (Fig. 8).**
  - Brightness, contrast, hue, grayscale, Gaussian blur.
  - Flip, rotation, crop, perspective.
  - JPEG at quality 40–90.
  - A combined attack (JPEG + crop 0.71 + brightness 0.5).
  - For video: H.264/H.265 at CRF {23, 30, 40, 50}.
- **Metrics.**
  - Bit accuracy and −log₁₀ p (Eq. 2).
  - PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CVVDP, and a "JND" quality regressor trained on internal user-study data (not reproducible).
  - **TPR at fixed FPR is not reported.**
- **Compute.** Training hardware and time are not reported.

## 4. Authors' findings (quoted from the paper)

- **Meta AI images (Table 1).** Results are given as bit accuracy / −log₁₀ p:

  | Condition | Pixel Seal |
  |---|---|
  | Identity | 1.00 / 75.8 |
  | Valuemetric | 0.97 / 68.1 |
  | Compression | 0.98 / 68.9 |
  | Geometric | 0.95 / 63.9 |
  | Combined | 0.91 / 50.3 |

  InvisMark reaches 0.94 / 22.1 on the combined attack, but it uses 94 effective bits.
- **Not uniformly best (our reading of Table 1).** The caption claims Pixel Seal "constantly outperforms". The table shows exceptions:
  - MBRS scores 1.00 / 75.2 on image compression.
  - Video Seal 1.0 scores 0.89 / 51.5 against 0.86 / 48.6 on MovieGen video compression.
  - InvisMark has higher combined bit accuracy on SA-1b (0.98 against 0.94).
- **Video codecs remain hard (Fig. 8, SA-V).** H.264 gives 0.77 at CRF 40 and 0.52 at CRF 50.
- **Imperceptibility (Table 2).** PSNR 48.9, SSIM 0.9905, CVVDP 9.87, LPIPS 0.0013, JND 0.34. Pixel Seal is top-3 on 4 of 5 metrics, but not first on most.
- **Ablations (Table 3).**
  - Adding MSE at λ = 1.0, using a fixed α = 0.2, or turning the discriminator on from the start each collapse bit accuracy to 0.50.
  - Removing JND drops PSNR to 43.1.
  - Training only at 256² lowers combined bit accuracy to 0.68.
- **Temporal pooling (Table 4, k = 4).** It gives up to a 2.2× speedup, with combined bit accuracy dropping from 0.68 to 0.66.

## 5. Practical limitations

**(a) Stated by the authors.** The paper has no limitations section. The trade-offs it mentions are:

- A stronger discriminator reduces robustness.
- The adversarial loss does not bound distortion, which is why α and the JND map are needed.
- Full-resolution JND and discriminator processing add cost.
- The maximum training side length is capped at 768.
- Video codecs remain hard.

**(b) Our assessment.**

- **Training is out of reach.** Reproducing training (600k × 256 high-resolution samples) is infeasible in a hackathon, so only inference and evaluation are realistic.
- **Parts of the evaluation are internal.** The JND quality metric and the Meta AI evaluation images cannot be reproduced.
- **The detection guarantee is unchecked.** It assumes clean images produce uniform random bits, and this is never verified empirically.
- **Some attack families are not evaluated.** These include diffusion/VAE regeneration, adversarial removal, forgery, and print-scan.
- **Checkpoint size.** The released checkpoint is about 1.24 GB. We have not downloaded it.

## 6. Official code (verified 2026-11-21, not cloned)

- **Repository.** [github.com/facebookresearch/videoseal](https://github.com/facebookresearch/videoseal), MIT license, actively maintained.
- **Pixel Seal support.**
  - Pixel Seal was added on 2026-01-21 (card `videoseal/cards/pixelseal.yaml`).
  - `videoseal.load("pixelseal")` auto-downloads a 1.24 GB checkpoint.
- **Card settings.**
  - nbits 256, processing size 256, scaling_w 0.2, attenuation `jnd_1_1`.
  - The embedder works on the Y channel only, unlike the 3-channel `w` described in the paper.
- **Bit extraction.** Message bits are `model.detect(x)["preds"][:, 1:] > 0`. Channel 0 is not a message bit.
- **Installation.**
  - The README targets Python 3.10, `pytorch==2.4.0` and CUDA 12.1, plus `requirements.txt`.
  - `decord` is a hard dependency, and the README warns it can be hard to install.
  - The PyPI release `videoseal` 1.0.1 predates Pixel Seal, so install from Git. We have not tested that install.
- **Evaluation.**
  - `python -m videoseal.evals.full --checkpoint <ckpt|baseline/...>` writes `metrics.csv`.
  - Attacks live in `videoseal/augmentation/` and metrics in `videoseal/evals/metrics.py`.
- **Training.**
  - `train.py` documents the **Video Seal** recipe. We found no adversarial-only Pixel Seal training config.
  - `docs/training.md` references a config path that does not exist at that location.

## 7. Reusable, task-independent components

1. **Bit accuracy.** `(1/n) Σ 1[m̂_k = m_k]`, where `m̂_k = 1[logit_k > 0]`. Implemented in `euroai.metrics.bit_accuracy`.
2. **Detection p-value (Eq. 2).** `p = Σ_{k=0}^{d} C(n,k) / 2^n`, where d is the Hamming distance. This equals `P[X ≥ n−d]` for `X ~ Bin(n, 1/2)`.
   - Implemented as `euroai.metrics.binomial_tail_pvalue(n - d, n)`.
   - Unit test: with n = 256 and d = 0, −log₁₀ p ≈ 77.06, which is the ceiling seen in Table 1.
3. **PSNR (code form).** `20·log10(255) − 10·log10(MSE_255)` per image. Implemented in `euroai.metrics.psnr`.
4. **JND heatmap (from `videoseal/modules/jnd.py`, not the paper).**
   - `Y = 255·(0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B)`.
   - Background luminance: `LA = conv5×5(Y, K)/32`.
   - Luminance masking: `la = 17(1 − √(LA/127))` if LA ≤ 127, else `3/128·(LA − 127) + 3`.
   - Contrast masking: `g = Sobel magnitude`, `cm = 0.117·16 g^2.4/(g² + 26²)`.
   - Combined map: `H = max(la + cm − 0.3·min(la, cm), 0)/255`.
5. **Training schedule.** The α schedule (Eq. 8, a quarter-cosine) and boosting `x̂_w = x + β(x_w − x)`.
6. **Straight-through estimator (Eq. 5)** for non-differentiable attacks.
7. **Deterministic attack suite** with the Fig. 8 parameters, using `torchvision.transforms.functional`. Fix the crop position or seed to make it deterministic.

## 8. Hackathon experiment ideas (ours, not the authors')

1. **Benchmark public checkpoints.** Run Pixel Seal and Video Seal 1.0 on about 200 public images under a deterministic attack suite.
   - Metrics: bit accuracy, −log₁₀ p, PSNR.
   - Risk: checkpoint size and CPU speed.
2. **Empirical false-positive check.** Run the extractor on at least 1000 clean images and compare the empirical FPR at p < 10⁻ᵏ with Eq. 2. Add a per-bit bias test.
   - Risk: estimating very small FPRs needs many images.
3. **Training-free regeneration attack.** Pass watermarked images through a VAE round-trip or a downscale+JPEG chain.
   - Metric: bit accuracy against PSNR to the original.
   - Risk: results depend on the VAE, and it needs a GPU.
4. **JND and α sweep.** Sweep `scaling_w` with attenuation on and off.
   - Metric: the bit-accuracy versus LPIPS curve.
   - Risk: turning JND off takes the model out of distribution.
5. **Tiny-scale schedule replication.** Train at 128² with 32 bits, comparing the three-stage schedule against a fixed α.
   - Risk: findings may not transfer to the paper's scale.
